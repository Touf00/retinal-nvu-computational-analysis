# Auditable IMACS host stroke-intervention and industrial-collaboration precedent

Date checked: 2026-10-14. Status: published historical evidence, **not confirmation of current availability**. This is a proposal screen, not a new animal experiment or validated AMOT/APJ PET study.

## Primary source and independently cross-checked bibliographic record

Garrigue P, Hache G, Bennis Y, et al. *Erythropoietin Pretreatment of Transplanted Endothelial Colony-Forming Cells Enhances Recovery in a Cerebral Ischemia Model by Increasing Their Homing Ability: A SPECT/CT Study*. Journal of Nuclear Medicine. 2016;57:1798–1804. DOI: 10.2967/jnumed.115.170308. PMID: 27609786.

- Primary article/abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27609786/
- Publisher full-text PDF: https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/57/11/1798.full.pdf
- Distinct earlier intervention precedent: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23816235/ (2013 EPO plus ECFC treatment after rat cerebral ischemia; **same research line, not an independent laboratory replication**).
- Complementary host imaging baseline: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26763027/ (2016 99mTc-HMPAO CBF, 99mTc-DTPA BBB, 99mTc-Annexin V-128 apoptosis in rat transient MCAO).

## What was actually tested

- Rat **1-hour transient MCAO followed by reperfusion**, with IV intervention **one day after stroke**.
- Three MCAO groups: PBS control **10 rats**, PBS-primed endothelial colony-forming cells (ECFCs) **13 rats**, and erythropoietin-primed ECFCs **10 rats**. These are animal group sizes reported in the abstract; do not multiply by imaging timepoints.
- SPECT/CT tracked ECFC homing, cerebral blood flow, BBB disruption and apoptosis up to day 14, with day-14 histological endpoints. This is **not PET** and **not AMOT/APJ binding validation**.
- The paper reported higher ischemic-hemisphere homing for EPO-primed versus PBS-primed ECFCs (146.5% ± 13.3% vs 111.0% ± 16.0%), reduced BBB disruption (control 387% ± 153%; ECFC-PBS 151% ± 46%; ECFC-EPO 112% ± 9%) and higher CBF ratio (control 72% ± 2%; ECFC-PBS 90% ± 4%; ECFC-EPO 99% ± 4%). **These are published hemisphere-activity ratio summaries, not independently reanalyzed individual-animal measurements.** Do not combine percentages with PET SUVs or claim an efficacy meta-analysis.
- Authors **Francesca Orlandi and Elena Castaldi** list **Advanced Accelerator Applications (Italy)** as affiliation in this publication. This is documented historical industrial participation, **not evidence they still work there or have agreed to mentor**.

## Decision relevance

1. There is a **named, published host-linked stroke intervention** rather than merely hypothetical drug exposure: EPO priming of transplanted ECFCs. It could provide a discussion lead for WP3, but its cell-production, transplantation and regulatory costs may make it inappropriate for a constrained 36-month PET fellowship. Do not insert it as a promised WP3 intervention.
2. There is a **documented historical nonacademic partner connection** (Advanced Accelerator Applications coauthors). This is a credible introduction lead for host discussion, not a signed IMACS commitment or a verified current industrial partner.
3. The host's published **CBF/BBB SPECT controls** are directly relevant to disentangling molecular PET uptake from permeability/perfusion. Their present availability and PET/SPECT scheduling are unknown.
4. **Critical separation:** ECFC surface CD146 used in cell-homing experiments is not proof that radiolabeled soluble CD146 specifically binds cerebral AMOT. Likewise the cell therapy's BBB effects cannot be attributed to AMOT/APJ tracer target engagement.
5. Before proposing an intervention, ask hosts whether any established rat stroke perturbation and associated imaging/assay resources remain active; prioritize an already available and affordable option. Keep **one model, one tracer, one conditional perturbation**.

## Audit boundaries

- No GEO or spatial sample mapping was changed. Existing RNA evidence remains distinct from PET and therapy.
- Independent replication: group n are individual rats **as described in the article abstract**; original animal-level measurements were not downloaded or statistically reanalyzed. Repeated scans are nested within animals.
- Compartment: ECFC cell-homing and BBB injury measures do not show blood-facing AMOT in human ischemic brain vessels or productive brain-parenchymal delivery.
- Species: rat transient MCAO; no human therapeutic trial or clinical PET target-binding inference.
- Provenance: source PMID 27609786 / DOI 10.2967/jnumed.115.170308; literature checked 2026-10-14. No new experimental outputs or code execution.
