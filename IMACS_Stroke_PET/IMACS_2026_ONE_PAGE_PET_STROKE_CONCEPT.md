# IMACS 2026 — one-page host discussion concept

**Working title:** Imaging neurovascular repair after ischemic stroke with established angiogenesis PET tracers

**Prospective host:** Philippe Garrigue and Benjamin Guillet, Aix-Marseille / CERIMED. **Scope:** one 36-month biological and molecular-imaging project. **Status:** proposal idea to shape jointly; no tracer/stroke-lab current availability verified.

## Clinical problem
Recanalization can restore major artery flow while microvascular repair remains heterogeneous. We lack a robust non-invasive way to distinguish transient vascular injury from reparative vessel remodeling and to predict delayed tissue recovery after stroke.

## What is new
Transfer an already published host-developed peripheral-ischemia angiogenesis PET platform to **brain ischemia**, without presupposing the tracer is usable in brain. Specifically test whether regional PET signal can capture *delayed* repair beyond tissue damage, perfusion disturbance or BBB leakage and whether that signal predicts later vascular/neurological recovery.

## Practical starting reagents
**Primary to test:** host [68Ga]Ga-sCD146 binds AMOT, validated in mouse hindlimb postischemic angiogenesis and myocardial infarct. **Backup only if justified by first pilot:** host [68Ga]Ga-AP747 targets APJ and was validated in peripheral ischemia. Do not assume both tracers are currently available or promise simultaneous development. The PET probe is sCD146 binding **AMOT**, not a probe for surface membrane CD146/MCAM.

## Central hypothesis and evidence risk
Prior GSE174574 3v3 MCAO/sham mouse EC and independent GSE223744 4v4 sorted-EC ischemia/naive analyses both show *acute AMOT and APLNR downregulation*. Apelin ligand RNA rises, which does not establish APJ protein. These observations do NOT support a claim that brain ischemic vessels already expose either target. Delayed vascular repair after stroke could involve a different protein state and microanatomical distribution; this is testable during the fellowship. Pooled day14 vs day2 data do **not** validate the hoped-for late increase. The first 6 months explicitly determine GO/NO-GO.

## Three work packages
**WP1, months 1–6: Brain tracer feasibility.** Confirm existing ligand synthesis/animal model, rat-versus-mouse target binding and radiochemical stability. Using pathologically defined rodent cerebral ischemic region/age samples where feasible, compare normal, acute and days 3/7/14 brain tracer autoradiography and isoform-/compartment-resolved IHC; show specific competitive blocking, vascular localization and tolerable normal brain background. **Milestone:** select ONE tracer for brain imaging, or reject and shift to host perfusion/BBB imaging without wasting the fellowship on de novo chemistry.

**WP2, months 6–20: Multimodal longitudinal imaging.** In ONE host-supported transient MCAO model with sham controls, image the selected tracer across pre-specified recovery times. Record dynamic PET input/blood pool, BBB permeability and cerebral perfusion with CERIMED-supported methods; assess imaging-to-vessel histological validation and avoid interpreting raw lesion SUV as target abundance. Longitudinal repeated measures are nested within animals; numbers and power follow actual pilot variability. **Milestone:** report brain-binding specificity, repeatability, lesion localization and temporal trajectory.

**WP3, months 20–30: Predictive and treatment-response value.** Compare early PET signal with subsequent perfusion, vascular maturation and neurological recovery, accounting for initial lesion severity and BBB damage. Compare reperfusion variants or **one already available, scientifically justified host intervention** if verified (no unpromised medication or collaborator). Explicitly separate drug receptor occupancy/ligand competition from genuine target change. **Milestone:** test whether molecular imaging adds quantitative prediction over conventional injury/perfusion measures. If PET probe is not feasible, the established quantitative perfusion/BBB imaging track provides an interpretable fallback.

Months 30–36: blinded pipeline finalization, imaging kinetics/reproducibility, reporting and sharing translational evidence. Plan the IMACS 3-month international academic secondment in PET kinetic modeling/imaging AI, and its required nonacademic mentorship in radiopharmacy, imaging instrumentation or biotech biomarkers **once actual partners are identified**.

## Why this pair of hosts?
They have published (1) **[68Ga]Ga-sCD146** postischemic regenerative PET (hindlimb/heart), (2) **[68Ga]Ga-AP747** molecular angiogenesis PET, and (3) **rat cerebral ischemia flow/BBB/apoptosis SPECT**. CERIMED lists microPET/SPECT, radiolabeling, autoradiography, histology and animal imaging. The proposal recombines demonstrated strengths in a new and falsifiable brain application, rather than promising to invent a radiotracer.

## Researcher training and transferable outputs
Hands-on stroke model/neurovascular experimental phenotyping; PET/SPECT and isotope radiochemistry QC; radiotracer affinity/biodistribution; blood input and kinetic modeling; multimodal registration; animal-level longitudinal statistics and imaging biomarker qualification. Applicable to molecular imaging, CNS biotech, radiopharma and preclinical CRO R&D.

## What to establish jointly — four questions, not external procurement
1. Is host [68Ga]Ga-sCD146 still synthesizable reproducibly? Would [68Ga]Ga-AP747 be easier for the first brain pilot?
2. Is rat tMCAO actively available with a clean sham group, or are mouse models better supported? Is rat target cross-reactivity established?
3. Which actual PET, blood-flow and BBB-permeability tools can be scheduled for paired longitudinal studies?
4. Which **one** therapy/reperfusion perturbation, three-month international academic partner and nonacademic mentor are already plausible within IMACS?

**Do NOT do now:** procure human ischemic FFPE, email brain banks, request new PI approval, create nine radiotracers, continue an exhaustive wet-lab protein validation campaign or produce a dry-lab meta-analysis as a prerequisite to application.
