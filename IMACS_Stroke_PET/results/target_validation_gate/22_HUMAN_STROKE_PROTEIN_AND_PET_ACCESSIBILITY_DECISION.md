# IMACS translational adjudication: ischemic human brain vessel proteins, ligand accessibility, and PET/BBB confounding

**Evidence search and archive:** 13 October 2026. **Scope:** nine frozen target hypotheses selected in `21_focused_protein_validation_hypotheses.md`. Primary evidence ledger: `22_human_ischemic_vascular_protein_and_tracer_evidence.csv`. Literature verification does **not** substitute for a new antibody-binding experiment; no new human protein stain, autoradiogram, ligand test or PET acquisition was carried out by this project.

## Decision in one paragraph

**ICAM1 and VCAM1 have direct published immunohistochemical human ischemic brain-vessel protein evidence.** They are the best entry point for a cerebral-vessel binding experiment, **not validated cerebral PET radiotracers**. The *mouse-stroke* vascular proteins MCAM/CD146 and PVR/CD155 have strong experimental support, **not yet verified as induced on human ischemic cerebral endothelium** in sources reviewed. Normal-human cerebral BBB protein is supported for **SLC3A2/CD98hc and TFRC/TfR1**, with functional BBB shuttle evidence in preclinical systems, **not human stroke-specific target upregulation**. APLNR/APJ and AMOT have existing peripheral-ischemia radiotracer chemistry, **not verified human stroke brain-vessel surface accessibility**. MAOB is an intracellular astrocyte-associated enzyme for a *different imaging modality*: human post-stroke PET exists with confounded THK5351, while the more MAO-B-selective SMBT-1 has human pharmacological occupancy/blocking data outside stroke.

**There is no PET-target GO for any of the nine on ischemic human brain-vessel evidence alone.** Separate diagnosis and delivery projects require different targets and endpoints.

## Explicit evidence grading (not a numerical composite)

- **Human ischemic brain-vessel protein:** **DIRECT** if actual infarcted human brain vessels were stained for the named protein; **NOT LOCATED** means only that no convincing primary human ischemic brain-vessel localization was found during this search, **not absence**.
- **Human normal brain-vessel protein:** human autopsy/surgical immunohistochemistry or isolated human microvessel protein quantification; does **not** establish human ischemic induction.
- **In vivo accessible binding:** proof of systemically dosed binder accumulating at the target in a relevant model, ideally with competition/knockout and a nonbinding control; IHC after permeabilization is **not** proof of blood-facing epitope.
- **PET proof:** isotope-bound ligand with specificity, biodistribution and kinetics **in that exact organism/disease/compartment**. A mouse MRI particle, optical aptamer, peripheral atherosclerosis PET, or tumor immunoPET cannot be relabeled as human stroke PET.

## Tracer-specific methodological failure modes

**Vascular-surface targets (ICAM1/VCAM1/MCAM/PVR/APJ/AMOT).**
1. **Perfusion and vascular volume.** Stroke changes vessel flow, hematocrit, recanalization and blood volume. A high lesion SUV can be a contrast from blood pool or delivery/clearance, not binding. Acquire dynamic images and image-derived/arterial plasma input; measure lesion and contralateral perfusion (ASL MRI or reference PET where appropriate).
2. **BBB leakage and extravascular retention.** Necrosis and disrupted junctions can allow an unrelated IgG/peptide to accumulate. Pair tracer with DCE-MRI permeability (where feasible), albumin/fibrinogen IgG extravasation in tissue and size-/PK-matched nonbinding tracer. Separate vascular perfused washout from extravascular retained activity. Human autopsy albumin distribution varies by vascular segment: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683449/
3. **Molecular specificity.** Use saturating blocking with **the same antigen** or knockout where feasible, nonbinding/isotype-matched labeled controls, competition with endogenous ligand, and ideally independent clone or epitope. Blocking can change pharmacokinetics, so monitor input/blood exposure. For VCAM1 measure **soluble VCAM1**; for ICAM1 exclude CD45+ leukocyte-associated activity.
4. **Cell source and vascular topology.** Perform co-labeling with ERG/CD31/CLDN5 (EC), PDGFRB/RGS5 (mural), GFAP (astroglia), CD45 (blood leukocytes), and albumin/IgG (leak). Analyze arterioles, capillaries and postcapillary venules separately. Histological abluminal/mural colocalization is not accessible from flowing blood across an intact BBB.
5. **Radiochemistry and kinetics.** Match 18F/68Ga to small-peptide/fragment PK and 89Zr to multi-day antibody PK as appropriate; do not assume a tracer established in peripheral tissue maintains brain target-to-blood contrast. Labeling must preserve affinity; determine radiochemical purity, molar activity, parent fraction/radiometabolites, clearance, non-specific uptake and radiation exposure.
6. **Residualizing antibody radionuclide risk.** 89Zr signal may persist after antibody degradation rather than reporting intact antibody/receptor occupancy; cf. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31147401/ and isotope-paired antibody studies https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acschemneuro.4c00552
7. **Human stroke PET cautionary example.** A real human stroke 18F-GE180 TSPO PET pilot reported strong dependence on BBB injury, vascular activity and nonspecific binding, limiting clinical applicability: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK553473/ . Molecular specificity must be demonstrated rather than assumed for the nine shortlisted targets.

**BBB therapeutic shuttles (CD98hc/TFRC) differ from vascular PET.** Demonstrate **intact cargo** in the capillary-depleted brain parenchyma and, separately, biochemical/functional target engagement in the intended cell. Quantify vascular retention, neuronal/glial uptake, Fc receptor interactions, endothelial endocytosis, non-specific IgG, normal organ distribution (bone marrow for TfR1; kidney, lacrimal/pancreas and other tissues for CD98hc), receptor competition and safety. Human normal BBB receptor availability does not mean stroke-specific enhancement.

**Parenchymal MAO-B PET differs from luminal vascular PET.** It is an intracellular mitochondrial target. Radioligand needs CNS penetration and sufficient unbound specific binding. Prefer validated selectivity/occupancy and kinetic modeling rather than interpreting infarct/perilesional THK5351 SUV as pure MAO-B. Compare blood flow, BBB permeability and age-related background; avoid equating astrocyte-endfoot acute RNA with protein months later.

## Proposed discriminating validation sequence for an actual IMACS experiment

**Gate 0 — experimental scope.** Select *either* a vascular PET feasibility track (ICAM1 + VCAM1 comparators; MCAM/PVR as follow-on) *or* host APJ/AMOT feasibility falsification *or* CD98hc/TfR delivery. Do not claim a three-year fellowship can develop all nine as tracers.

**Gate 1 — human tissue surface protein and anatomy.** Obtain ethics-approved, clinically annotated human ischemic stroke autopsy/biobank sections (core, border, matched uninfarcted) with stroke-to-death interval, fixation, reperfusion status and age/vascular risk control. Quantify ICAM1/VCAM1/CD31/ERG/CLDN5 jointly, separately from GFAP/CD45/PDGFRB cells; normalized per vessel length/surface/EC count rather than per arbitrary field. Include positive and negative antibody tissue controls and immunogen/independent clone if possible. This measures protein locality, **not** blood-side epitope access.

**Gate 2 — blood-facing binder access.** On intact ex vivo cerebral microvessels or tissue-preparation-compatible microfluidic brain vessel models, compare target-binding at nonpermeabilized luminal surface; show temperature/time dependence, blocking, cross-reactivity, affinity and fluorescent binder penetration. If human vessels cannot be obtained, verify in ischemic mouse by intravenously injecting bound probe prior to vascular perfusion and checking capillary/mural localization under equal exposure conditions. **STOP** if signal only emerges after permeabilization, hemorrhagic leakage, or absent normal-organ specificity.

**Gate 3 — paired-target PET feasibility.** Start with a validated commercially available binder/antibody fragment and appropriate chelator, not bespoke therapeutic synthesis in parallel. Quantify in vitro specific binding, plasma metabolites, 1- or multiple-time-point blood pool correction, dynamic PET time–activity, nonbinding control, competition, perfusion and permeability controls, organ uptake. Compare stroke **versus sham-operated** rodents and ipsilateral/contralateral sides across independently randomized *animals*, at acute and subacute timepoints; a strong in-vitro surface signal alone does not authorize in-vivo target claim.

**Gate 4 — translational decision.** A PET imaging GO requires binding specificity *and* an interpretable lesion-to-blood/normal-tissue contrast under matched flow/leakage, reproducible across animals, plus credible human ischemic vessel epitope. A BBB shuttle GO instead requires intact cargo delivery beyond capillaries and pharmacological target engagement, not total radiolabel in brain.

**Not a negative biological conclusion:** No direct human ischemic vessel protein report located for seven tracks during this scoped review does not mean the protein is absent, undruggable, unexpressed or inaccessible. It identifies the missing experiment to run.

## Data and provenance boundaries

Our archived IMACS transcriptome results showed 6 human paired infarct-core vs contralateral **whole-tissue** donors (none of 27 mapped genes survived 27-panel FDR); six adult temporal-lobe surgical-control FACS EC preparations (not human stroke); mouse endothelial stroke models with distinct controls. None of these RNA measures establishes the named protein as exposed on *human ischemic brain vessel luminal surfaces*. The attached evidence table explicitly distinguishes each level. Spatial lesion labels await original author metadata and are not needed to audit the primary protein/ligand premise.
